# DME catheter case trace — 29 October 2026

The strongest research path is the **secondary-payer cash trail and the effectiveness of suspension notifications**. This is a documented control problem with published prosecutions, not a newly discovered fraud case. No reward entitlement or new recoverable amount has been established.

## What the cases establish

The original Gold Rush indictment describes an alleged network of more than 30 DME suppliers, nominee owners, couriers, common service providers and overseas transfers. Its described conduct period is approximately August 2022–September 2024. The alleged roles are recorded individually in CT-F002; a service-provider relationship does not establish culpability. [CT-S003]

DOJ expressly connects the later **Centennial Med Supply LLC** and **ND Medical Solutions LLC** cases to the same alleged organization. That establishes a published organizational allegation; it does not establish that their later claims are already included in the original $10.6bn figure. No totals from these branches are added here. [CT-S006, CT-S010]

## Concrete unresolved questions

1. **Notification effectiveness.** GAO says suspension reports were delivered in December 2025 and January 2026, with monthly reporting planned. Claims remained processed/approved while Medicare held cash. Test when each insurer received and applied a suspension, and distinguish claims adjudicated before notice from cash released afterward. The old absence of reporting cannot be described as unchanged. [CT-S005]
2. **Centennial's unnamed counterparties.** Affidavit paragraph47 records approximately $477,807 inbound across five checks and $284,000 outbound across three checks involving two other unnamed DME companies. The investigators explicitly left purpose unresolved. Seek later unsealed filings; do not infer counterparties from matching addresses or approximate amounts. [CT-S009 p18]
3. **Other federal payer exposure.** The affidavit identifies approximately 4,873 CHAMPVA claims billed at $10.9m, without establishing cash paid. This warrants a payer-specific reconciliation, not a claimed loss. [CT-S009 p13 fn4]
4. **Program migration.** CMS reports four of its eleven suppliers moved from revoked Original Medicare billing to Medicare Advantage. This supports testing revocation-to-preclusion delays, but the supplier names are absent and must not be guessed. [CT-S012]

For each question, the decisive evidence is a matched sequence of provider identity, effective ownership, service date, adjudication, suspension, notice receipt, cash disbursement, reversal and recovery. Public annual aggregates cannot supply that sequence. Legitimate claim runout, administrative delay, mismatched periods and ordinary commercial transfers must be tested before alleging wrongdoing.

## Verification and limits

Twenty-one source records include original DOJ-hosted EDNY, Bendeliani and Danelia filings, plus the mirrored Gugava filing. Source files were downloaded and hashed in scratch; this repository contains source metadata and minimal factual extracts, not personal/contact/bank-account details. OCR was used for scanned filings and key monetary pages were visually inspected. No authenticated court-host Gugava copy or complete current docket export was obtained. Exact source locators and SHA256 values are in `evidence_records.json`.

The earlier OIG catheter audit covers July 2021–June 2022 and estimates improper documentation/payment issues. It is not a fraud conviction or the same cohort. The August 2026 OIG white paper supports ownership, order and identifier control work; it does not independently establish the identity of unnamed suppliers. [CT-S013, CT-S014]

The next pass should prioritize the four questions above, exact provider crosswalks and latest docket dispositions. No person was contacted, no complaint filed, no payment requested, and no novel named accusation generated.
