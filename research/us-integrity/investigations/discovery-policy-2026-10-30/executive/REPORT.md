# Executive reward policy: speech, implementation and unresolved conflicts

Checked **30 October 2026**. The verified written WHIP program offers discretionary **$10,000–$30,000** awards at the criminal charging stage. It does **not** implement a universal right to 1% of savings. The public descriptions differ from the written program on the formula, triggering event and decision maker. Preserve those differences rather than merging them into a revenue assumption. [EX-S04–07]

## What was said, and how securely it is established

The White House identifies the announcement as **20 October 2026**; its photo captions locate it in the Oval Office. Its official video page exists. [EX-S01–02] Exact excerpts and timestamps are in [statements.json](statements.json), EX-ST02–08. Their source is **Roll Call Factba.se, a non-government transcript**. The official embedded video could not be retrieved for independent audio verification, and a government-issued written transcript was not located in the bounded search. Do not describe this as a verified official transcript.

The transcript attributes an uncapped percentage to Trump, ties it to **recovered money**, and describes presidential reward arbitration. Vance describes broader-than-criminal eligibility. Those propositions conflict with current DOJ guidance. Vance’s separate assertion about more than 99% of discovered fraud falling outside the False Claims Act is recorded as an attributed assertion, with no supporting denominator or coverage analysis found here. Ferguson’s legislative proposals are referrals for congressional verification, not evidence of enacted legislation. [EX-S03]

This internal inconsistency remains unresolved. Coverage of an agency’s programs or benefits should not be generalized into reward coverage of every defense purchase. [EX-S05–07]

The congressional report separately examines the statute cited by DOJ and its reward limits. A speech, a directive, an executive order, program guidance and a congressional enactment occupy different authority categories. [EX-S05, EX-S08–09]

## Implications for discovering new cases

Public-data analysis can be relevant: the terms expressly contemplate analysis alone or with others. However, it must reveal information not generally known. The text separately requires information previously unknown to DOJ and material additional information; an existing related investigation is not automatically disqualifying. [EX-S05] The FAQ rejects simple repetition and identical later tips, limits claimants to individuals, and preserves eligibility restrictions. [EX-S06]

No blanket U.S. citizenship or residence requirement was located in the reviewed WHIP terms or FAQ. The text does exclude specified law-enforcement personnel and elected/appointed foreign government officials who acquired the information in that status. This bounded textual finding does **not** determine a UK-based researcher’s eligibility or payment treatment. [EX-S05–06, EX-ST23]

Implement these project controls:

- Record the analyst’s new inference separately from the public facts supporting it; record how it could be disproved.
- Track public-case searches as bounded checks, never proof that the government lacks knowledge.
- Store rewards as `not_assessed`; keep estimated savings, alleged losses, collected recoveries and awards in separate fields.
- Route a concrete, reviewed evidence packet through the relevant program’s required process. No report was submitted in this research.

These are research design recommendations, not additional agency eligibility rules. The White House dashboard itself separates identified amounts, annualized prevention and enforcement descriptions. Its retrieved rendering did not provide a reconciled cash ledger. Multiplying those aggregates by 1% would create an unsupported estimate. [EX-S10]
